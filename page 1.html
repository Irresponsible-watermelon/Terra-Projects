Nobody Expects the Spanish Inquisition

The Spanish Inquisition was a radical proselytizing Catholic movement that spread alongside the Spanish Empire during much of its history.

The Origins of the Spanish Inquisition

The Spanish Inquisition was established in 1478 by Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile. It was intended to maintain Catholic orthodoxy in their kingdoms and to replace the medieval Inquisition.

Societal Responses to the Spanish Inquisition

The Spanish Inquisition was met with mixed responses from society. Some supported it as a means of maintaining religious unity, while others criticized it for its harsh methods and persecution of non-Catholics.

Are we right about the Spanish Inquisition?

While the Spanish Inquisition is often remembered for its brutality, it is important to think about why it is remembered in this way.
According to Ed Condon, the editor and a cofounder of The Pillar and a Media Fellow at the Institute for Human Ecology at the Catholic University of America, this viewpoint has been shaped in part by attempts to propagandise the institution.

much of our popular conception of [The Spanish Inquisition] is the product of Elizabethan propaganda and gothic fiction. There was a concerted effort by northern (mostly Protestant) European kingdoms to paint the Spanish Empire as constitutionally evil; not just a political, religious, and military rival but an existential threat to all that was good in the world.
